My grandfather Mateo owned a tiny radio repair shop beside the old market in Guadalajara. In the summer of 1987, when I was nine, he let me work there every Saturday. I could not repair anything, so my job was to label the small envelopes of screws and to water the yellow marigolds outside his window. At noon, he always tuned the radio to a bolero station, made two cups of cinnamon tea, and told me about the first bicycle he rebuilt from discarded parts. He called that red bicycle La Valiente, the brave one. Years later, he gave it to me and said, "A broken thing is only a story waiting for patient hands." I still keep one of his empty screw envelopes in my toolbox. Whenever I smell cinnamon, I am nine years old again, sitting on that wooden stool while the radios hummed and the marigolds leaned toward the sun.
